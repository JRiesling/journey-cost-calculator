Why do gas prices vary so much by state?

The biggest factor is state fuel taxes, which range from around 9 cents per gallon in Alaska to over 68 cents per gallon in California. Proximity to Gulf Coast refineries also plays a major role — states in the South and Midwest benefit from shorter distribution chains, while West Coast states face higher costs due to limited pipeline access and stricter environmental regulations that require specially blended fuel.

Crude oil makes up roughly 51% of the price of a gallon of gas in the US, with taxes accounting for 17%, distribution and marketing 18%, and refining costs 14%, according to the US Energy Information Administration.

How speed affects your gas bill

Driving faster uses significantly more fuel. At highway speeds, aerodynamic drag increases with the square of your speed — meaning going from 65mph to 80mph doesn't just add 23% more distance covered per hour, it increases fuel consumption by considerably more than that. Most vehicles achieve their best fuel economy between 45mph and 60mph.

On a cross-country drive from New York to LA, driving at 75mph instead of 65mph could add $30-50 to your gas bill — on top of the speeding ticket risk.

US vs UK gas prices — how do they compare?

American drivers pay significantly less for gas than their UK counterparts. At $4.00 per gallon (roughly £3.20/gallon or 70p/litre), US gas is about half the price of UK petrol at around 157p/litre. The main reason is taxation — UK fuel duty and VAT add around 85p to every litre, while US federal gas tax is just 18.4 cents per gallon with varying state taxes on top.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average gas price in the US in 2026?

The national average for regular unleaded is around $4.00 per gallon as of mid-2026, up from around $3.30 a year earlier. California is the most expensive state at $5.46/gallon; Indiana is the cheapest at $3.37/gallon.

How do I calculate the gas cost of a road trip?

Why is gas so expensive in California?

California has the highest state gas taxes in the US, strict environmental regulations requiring a unique fuel blend, limited pipeline connections to Gulf Coast refineries, and high distribution costs. These combine to push prices consistently $1-2 above the national average.

What's the cheapest state for gas?

Southern and Midwestern states consistently have the cheapest gas — Indiana, Oklahoma, Texas, and Mississippi regularly come in well below the national average due to lower state taxes and proximity to Gulf Coast refineries.
