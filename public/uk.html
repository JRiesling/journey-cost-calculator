UK speed limits and fuel costs

UK national speed limits are 60 mph on A-roads and 70 mph on motorways. Driving above these limits doesn't just risk a fine — it significantly increases your fuel costs and CO2 emissions.

On a typical 200-mile motorway journey:

Driving at 80 mph instead of 70 mph costs around £2-3 more in fuel and produces roughly 4 kg more CO2 — but only saves around 14 minutes. For most drivers, that's not a trade-off worth making.

Driving at 60 mph saves around £1-2 compared to 70 mph, but adds around 17 minutes. Good for long journeys where time isn't critical.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to drive per mile in the UK?
At current petrol prices (156.8p/litre) and an average of 40 MPG, fuel costs approximately 8.9p per mile. For diesel (188.8p/litre) at 45 MPG, it's around 9.5p per mile. These figures exclude insurance, tax, and maintenance.
Is it cheaper to drive or take the train?
For solo travel, driving is often cheaper than the train, especially with advance booking. With passengers, driving becomes significantly cheaper per person. For journeys into central London, the train is usually more cost-effective when you factor in parking.
Why is diesel so much more expensive than petrol in the UK?
Diesel is currently around 32p per litre more expensive than petrol — one of the largest gaps in Europe. The CMA has found evidence of "rocket and feather" pricing in diesel, where prices rise quickly but fall slowly. The government's Fuel Finder scheme is designed to improve transparency.
What is the most fuel-efficient speed to drive at in the UK?
Most petrol cars achieve their best fuel economy between 45-60 mph. At 70 mph efficiency drops around 5%, and at 80 mph it drops around 13% compared to 60 mph. The exact figures vary by vehicle — newer, more aerodynamic cars handle higher speeds more efficiently.
How do I find the cheapest petrol near me?
The UK Government's Fuel Finder scheme (mandatory since February 2026) requires all forecourts to report prices within 30 minutes of any change. Apps like Fuel Finder UK and PetrolPrices use this data to show live prices near you. Supermarkets typically offer the best prices — around 5p per litre cheaper than branded forecourts on average.
How much is fuel duty in the UK?
Fuel duty is currently 52.95p per litre for both petrol and diesel. It was cut by 5p per litre in March 2022 in response to rising prices following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, and has been frozen since. VAT at 20% is also charged on the full pump price including the duty.
