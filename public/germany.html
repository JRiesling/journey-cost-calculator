The Autobahn — what UK drivers need to know

Germany's Autobahn network is famous worldwide, but several myths surround it. Here's what's actually true:

Road typeSpeed limitNotes
Autobahn (unrestricted sections)No limit (advisory 130 km/h)~30% of the network has no limit
Autobahn (restricted sections)Varies (100–130 km/h)Near cities, construction zones
Rural roads (Bundesstraße)100 km/h (62mph)
Built-up areas50 km/h (31mph)30 km/h in residential zones

Importantly, the Autobahn is free for cars — Germany does not charge tolls on its motorway network for private vehicles. Heavy goods vehicles pay a distance-based toll (Maut) but this doesn't apply to cars or campervans.

💡 Tips to save money on fuel in Germany

🕐 Fill up in the evening — German fuel prices follow a predictable daily cycle, with prices typically lowest between 18:00 and 22:00 and highest in the morning.
🛒 Use supermarket forecourts — Aldi, Lidl, and Rewe have fuel stations that are typically 3–8 cents/L cheaper than branded stations.
📱 Use the Clever Tanken app — Germany's most popular fuel price comparison app, showing live prices at all stations near you.
🇱🇺 Fill up in Luxembourg or Poland — if your route takes you near the border, both countries have significantly cheaper fuel than Germany.
🚗 Drive at 130 km/h — sticking to the advisory speed limit rather than pushing on saves significant fuel on Autobahn sections with no posted limit.

Frequently asked questions

How much does petrol cost in Germany in 2026?

Around €1.59 per litre for Super E10 petrol and €1.41/L for diesel — slightly above the EU average but cheaper than France, the Netherlands, and the Nordic countries.

Is the Autobahn really unlimited speed?

Partly. Around 30% of the Autobahn network has no permanent speed limit, with an advisory speed of 130 km/h. The remaining 70% has posted limits, particularly near cities, in construction zones, and in adverse weather conditions.

Do I have to pay tolls on German motorways?

No — German motorways are free for cars and motorcycles. Only heavy goods vehicles (trucks) pay a distance-based Maut toll. This makes Germany unusually good value for long-distance road trips compared to France or Austria.

What fuel should I use in Germany?

Super E10 is the standard unleaded petrol — equivalent to E10 in the UK. Super Plus (E5, 98 RON) is also widely available. Diesel is labeled Diesel everywhere. LPG (Autogas) is available at many stations if your vehicle is converted.
