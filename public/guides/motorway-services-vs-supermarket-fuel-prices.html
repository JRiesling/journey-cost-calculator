Motorway Services vs Supermarket Fuel Prices

⏱️ 4 min read✍️ Fuel SmarterMid-November 2026 figures

Motorway services charge 24.0p more per litre for petrol and 18.6p more for diesel than supermarket forecourts. On a 50-litre fill that is about £12.00 extra for petrol or £9.30 extra for diesel.

Motorway services are the most expensive place to buy fuel in the UK. We work out the averages from the government's Fuel Finder data, which covers thousands of forecourts, and the figures below update automatically as prices change.

Why is fuel dearer at motorway services?

It is generally put down to a captive market. Drivers on a motorway have few alternatives within easy reach, so there is little competition to push prices down, and operators pay high rents and run sites 24 hours a day. Supermarkets, by contrast, often sell fuel to bring shoppers in and compete hard with the forecourts nearby.

How to avoid the premium

  • Fill up before you join the motorway, ideally at a supermarket or a cheap station near your route.
  • If you need fuel on a long journey, leave at a junction and look for a nearby supermarket rather than using the services.
  • Use the Fuel & Charging Finder to find the cheapest stations along your route before you set off.
  • Use motorway services for rest, food and toilets, which is what they are best at, and not as your main fuel stop.
  • Never let it get critically low, though. A planned stop at the services is better than running out.

Where the figures come from

Prices come from the UK government's Fuel Finder scheme, where retailers report their pump prices. Each average is a simple average across stations of that type, so it is not weighted by how much fuel each station sells. Motorway services are the stations the scheme flags as motorway service stations, and supermarkets are those it flags as supermarket forecourts. Everything else is grouped as "other stations".
