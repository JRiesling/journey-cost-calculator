Cheapest Hybrid Cars to Run in the UK (2026)

⏱️ 5 min read✍️ Fuel SmarterUpdated November 2026

With UK petrol averaging 174.1p a litre, a hybrid can cut a typical fuel bill by a third or more compared with a petrol car of the same size. This guide ranks popular full hybrids (the self-charging kind, no plug) by what they cost to fuel, using current prices.

How we worked these out: official combined MPG figures come from manufacturers and car-buying sites, and real drivers typically get around 15% less. We've applied that to every car, then priced the fuel at the current UK petrol average of 174.1p per litre. Treat the figures as a fair comparison between cars, not a promise for your own driving.

How much does a hybrid save?

A petrol car managing a real-world 38 MPG costs about £20.83 per 100 miles to fuel at today's prices. The most efficient hybrids in the table cost around £13.19-£15.52, which is a saving of roughly £741 a year for someone driving 12,000 miles.

Where hybrids shine: town and suburban driving, where the battery helps most. On long motorway runs at 70mph the advantage shrinks, and a good diesel can match them. If most of your miles are motorway, check both.

Hybrid, plug-in hybrid or electric?

A full hybrid is the simplest: no plug, no charging habits to change. A plug-in hybrid can be cheaper still if you charge it at home, but if you rarely plug in it behaves like a heavy petrol car. If you can charge at home, a pure EV is usually cheapest of all — see our cheapest electric cars to run and the EV vs hybrid comparison.
