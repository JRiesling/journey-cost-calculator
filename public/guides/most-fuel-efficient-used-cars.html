Most Fuel Efficient Used Cars UK 2026

The used car market has never offered better fuel efficiency. These are the models worth seeking out — from budget petrols to nearly-new hybrids.

Updated June 2026  ·  10 min read

Buying used is often the smartest way to get a fuel-efficient car. Depreciation means you can get a three or four-year-old hybrid for significantly less than the new price, while benefiting from much the same real-world economy. With petrol at around £1.44 per litre in 2026, the fuel savings from a 60mpg used hybrid versus a 35mpg older petrol car add up to several hundred pounds per year.

Real-world MPG figures for used cars are typically 10–15% lower than official WLTP figures. For hybrids, the gap is smaller — their efficiency is most pronounced in everyday urban driving, which closely mirrors the test conditions.

Top used cars for fuel efficiency by budget

#1 Overall Used from ~£12,000

Toyota Yaris Hybrid (2020–present)

55–65mpg real-world

The third-generation Yaris Hybrid (from 2020) is the standout used buy for fuel efficiency. Real-world economy of 55–65mpg is consistently achievable in everyday driving, particularly on urban and suburban routes where the electric motor does the bulk of the work. Toyota's hybrid system is self-charging — no plugging in required — and the brand's reliability record means these cars hold up well over high mileage.

The 2020–2022 cars are now appearing in good supply on the used market at £12,000–£16,000, representing excellent value. Check service history carefully and look for a full Toyota dealer service record.

#2 Used from ~£14,000

Toyota Corolla Hybrid (2019–present)

52–62mpg real-world

For buyers wanting a larger, more practical car, the Corolla Hybrid is the best used buy for combined efficiency and reliability. The 1.8-litre hybrid version delivers 52–62mpg in real-world mixed driving. Available as both a hatchback and the more practical Touring Sports estate, both hold their value well and are increasingly available from Toyota Approved Used programmes.

The Touring Sports estate is particularly worth seeking out — it's one of the most practical and efficient used estates on the market at any price point.

Real-world MPG: 52–62mpg Used price: £14,000–£24,000 Generation: 2019–present Best version: 1.8 Hybrid (more frugal than 2.0)

#3 Used from ~£8,000

Hyundai Ioniq Hybrid (2016–2022)

50–60mpg real-world

The original Ioniq Hybrid (now discontinued in favour of the electric Ioniq range) is one of the best-value used buys for fuel efficiency. Its aerodynamic body delivers genuinely strong economy at motorway speeds — unusual for a hybrid — and 50–60mpg in mixed driving is achievable. Earlier cars (2016–2019) are available from around £8,000 and represent outstanding value per mile.

The driving experience is unspectacular but smooth, and reliability has been strong. Check for any outstanding recalls and confirm the 12V auxiliary battery has been serviced.

#4 Used from ~£6,000

Toyota Prius (2016–present)

50–65mpg real-world

The Prius remains one of the most efficient used cars available at any price. Third and fourth-generation models (from 2016) deliver 50–65mpg in everyday use, with urban drivers often exceeding 70mpg. The Prius has a deserved reputation for mechanical durability — many taxi fleet cars cover 300,000+ miles on their original hybrid batteries. For buyers who prioritise economy above all else, a well-maintained used Prius is hard to beat.

Used hybrid tip: Don't be put off by higher-mileage Toyota hybrids. The hybrid battery is designed for the life of the car, and Toyota's powertrain warranty (on approved used cars) can extend peace of mind. A 100,000-mile Corolla Hybrid with a full service history is a better buy than a 40,000-mile model with gaps in its paperwork.

What to check before buying a used hybrid

  • Service history: Look for a full dealer or main dealer service record. Hybrid-specific servicing intervals differ slightly from conventional cars.
  • Auxiliary 12V battery: Most hybrids have a small 12V battery alongside the main hybrid battery. These need replacing every 4–6 years and are a common cause of issues in older used hybrids.
  • Hybrid battery health: Reputable dealers will have checked this. On a test drive, watch for the battery depleting unusually quickly or the petrol engine cutting in at very low speeds where it normally wouldn't.
  • Recall status: Check the DVLA vehicle enquiry service for any outstanding recalls. Toyota has had several recall programmes over the years — ensure these have been completed.
  • Mileage vs condition: A higher-mileage car with full history often beats a lower-mileage one with service gaps, especially for hybrids.
Diesel used car caution: If you're considering a used diesel, check whether the car has a diesel particulate filter (DPF). Short, urban journeys don't allow the DPF to regenerate properly, which can lead to expensive problems. Used diesels suit high-mileage, motorway-heavy driving; for urban use, a used hybrid is nearly always the better choice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most fuel efficient used car to buy in the UK in 2026?

For overall efficiency and reliability, the Toyota Yaris Hybrid (2020 onwards) is our top pick. For the best value per pound spent, the Hyundai Ioniq Hybrid (from around £8,000) is hard to beat.

Are used hybrid batteries reliable?

Yes — particularly for Toyota. The main hybrid battery in Toyota and Hyundai hybrids is designed for the life of the car and rarely fails before 150,000–200,000 miles. The small auxiliary 12V battery is a more common issue and costs around £80–£150 to replace.

Is a used diesel or used hybrid better for fuel economy?

It depends on your driving. For urban and mixed driving, a used hybrid consistently wins. For predominantly motorway use at high mileage, a modern used diesel (particularly the Octavia TDI) is competitive and sometimes cheaper to buy.
